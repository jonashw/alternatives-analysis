Situation: A test of the new ranker on a ten-criterion decision. The first run took 23 answers and collapsed into one ten-way tie. With four must-haves sorted into gates, six wants remained, and two of them overlapped (a fallback path vs. the normal path, and a fallback that is also a copy).
Jon said: "sometimes I struggle to grasp the true meaning of a criterion when I see its shortened phrasing over and over" and "I'm also struggling at times when two criteria appear to be linked or dependent... which can influence my choices."
Jon said, rejecting "picture two options equal on everything else": "When two criteria are linked, it can be impossible to picture them as two mutually exclusive options."
Jon said, after the reworded run: "the way a specific item was held constant at the beginning ("when _ works") helped me to understand what actually varied, the "I" statements helped me to see myself in them. I felt more confident about this session than others." and "This was the biggest improvement."
Jon said: "'I' am the measurer of value here. In other situations (professional), the organization I'm working for measures it, and I am but a proxy. In those situations, I expect the 'I' statements to become 'the org' or 'the actual user'." (typos fixed)
Observed: the reworded run needed 12 answers, no big tie, and its ranking changed from the run before (META moved from third to first), so the wording moved the answers.
Inferred (my reading): overlap between criteria is the analyst's job to remove before ranking; a mental trick handed to the owner does not substitute for wording that keeps each comparison to one quality.
